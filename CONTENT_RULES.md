# Content Rules

How I write and what I post. The visual side is in [`DESIGN_RULES.md`](DESIGN_RULES.md). The same principle applies: **neutral, natural, specific, minimal.**

## Voice

- Plain and direct. Say what it is, how it works, and why it matters.
- Specific over impressive: concrete nouns, real examples, honest trade-offs.
- Short sentences, active voice, first person where it's my own experience.
- Calm and technical. Not hype, not motivational, not salesy.
- Sound like an engineer explaining something to a colleague.

## What to post

Post ideas should come from real work and real learning. Good sources:

- A bug I debugged: symptom, cause, fix, what I'd check first next time.
- A trade-off I made: options considered, why one won, what it cost.
- A concept explained from first principles, with a small correct example.
- A performance or architecture change and the measured result.
- A mistake or misconception I corrected.
- Something I built, with the real constraint that shaped it.

Each post answers one question: **what would a working engineer learn from this in 60 seconds?**

## Structure

1. **Hook:** one plain line stating the claim or the problem. No clickbait.
2. **Body:** 3 to 8 short lines or one small code block. One idea.
3. **Close:** the takeaway, or one honest question. No "thoughts?" bait, no "follow for more".

Length: shorter is better. Cut anything that doesn't add information.

## Numbers and claims

- Only use numbers I can back up. Never invent metrics, percentages, repo counts, years or results.
- If a claim needs a metric and I don't have one, describe the change qualitatively or measure it first.
- Don't exaggerate scope or ownership. Say what I did.

## Anti-slop (writing)

Avoid:

- Buzzwords: cutting-edge, next-gen, game-changing, revolutionize, supercharge, unlock, seamless, leverage, empower, delve, dive into, landscape, journey, "in today's fast-paced world".
- Patterns that read as machine-written: "It's not X, it's Y", rule-of-three lists for rhythm, rhetorical questions as hooks, "Here's the thing:", "Let that sink in", heavy em-dashes, tidy summary lines that restate the post.
- Emoji, emoji bullets, hashtag walls (at most 0 to 2 relevant hashtags), exclamation marks.
- Fake storytelling ("I was fired and then..."), engagement bait, "agree?" endings.
- Unsourced statistics and vague authority ("studies show").

## Post checklist

1. Is it true, and can I defend every claim in an interview?
2. Does it teach or show one specific thing?
3. Would I say this out loud to a colleague?
4. Is there anything here that only exists to sound impressive? Cut it.
5. Does the graphic follow `DESIGN_RULES.md` and match the post's single idea?

## Bio, headline and descriptions

- Headline: what I do and the kind of systems I work on. No "passionate", "ninja", "rockstar", "guru".
- Descriptions on links, repos and cards: one factual sentence.
- Keep the same wording for the same thing across LinkedIn, GitHub, the portfolio and the blog.
